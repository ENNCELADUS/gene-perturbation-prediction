The exp05 GeneEffect backbone Done

A pretrained single-cell perturbation backbone (Arc STATE) is turned into a differentiable virtual cell: a trainable perturbation adapter injects a query gene, STATE forwards the basal K562 cell set into a predicted transcriptome, and a light head reads it out as a single-gene DepMap GeneEffect — a relative growth-rate effect, not a cell-death label and never a double-knockout quantity.

Basal K562 non-targeting cells enter STATE as a side input, so the predicted response is cell-state–specific. Training supervises on observed perturbation expression and DepMap labels; at inference the whole model is frozen and produces ĉ per gene. The head is a moment-pool + diagonal-GMM occupancy summary into a small MLP — the component that later carries the cross-cell-line failure (§2).

02

Cross-cell-line generalization In progress · no results yet

The HCT116 audit localized the problem: the encoder-level dependency prior is conserved, but the K562-fit readout does not transfer. So the current build asks a sharper question — can a single-gene GeneEffect backbone be made genuinely cell-line-specific? — by fetching more cell lines and rebuilding the parts that collapsed. Everything below is planned/under-construction; no number here is a result.

① More cell lines — train on some, hold out others

A context→fitness function needs many contexts. We assemble a multi-line Perturb-seq + DepMap universe with strictly separated roles, then test leave-one-cell-line-out and true held-out lines.

Tahoe enters only as basal (unperturbed) single cells supplying context breadth — no drug perturbation, no compound token. Held-out lines never inform training, preprocessing, or slice selection.

② Architecture improvements

  • Stronger encoder — Tx1-3B (frozen). Replace the per-cell HVG/ESM2 input with Tahoe-pretrained cell embeddings; its pretraining on ~50 cancer lines is the a-priori edge for held-out cancer lines. An HVG-ST control isolates encoder gains from head gains.
  • Drop the K562-fit GMM + MSE head. That is the exact part that collapsed on HCT116. Rebuild with a permutation-invariant moment pool + a head conditioned on the basal embedding — inductive, with no one-hot line ID (a one-hot has no slot for an unseen line).
  • Rank / correlation loss + variance-matching. Pure MSE is won by predicting the line mean — precisely the observed collapse — so the objective is rank-correlation with an explicit variance-preservation term.
  • Few-shot adaptation. Context is inductive, so zero-shot is well-defined; k labels from a held-out line drive a light affine/head-only calibration, reported as an accuracy-vs-k curve.

What success looks like — the single kill test

Primary metric: paired rank-correlation on the differentially-essential slice (genes whose GeneEffect varies across lines — slice fixed on training lines only), per held-out line. The rebuilt model must beat "copy-K562 + k labels" with a per-line 95% CI excluding zero, at a pre-registered k. Baselines: copy-K562, cross-line mean, nearest-line, lineage-only, plain CCLE-bulk→GeneEffect regression — each with its own k-shot correction.

Claim boundary Even a strong result here is backbone transfer, not synthetic lethality. It is single-gene GeneEffect across lines; it opens no pairwise labels in any untouched line and makes no cross-cell-line SL claim. It is the substrate the bridge in §3 would compose — nothing more.
03

From GeneEffect to SL: the bridge Discussion · Bridge A paused

Sections 1–2 give us single-gene GeneEffect, one gene at a time. Synthetic lethality is a pairwise, second-order quantity. This is the conceptual gap — and the first-principles reason two single GeneEffects, on their own, can never bridge it.

The definition — GI is a mixed second difference

Let f(a,b) be fitness over two perturbation switches, WT = f(0,0). Single-mutant fitness is a first-order (gradient) quantity; the genetic interaction is second-order (curvature):

GIₐᵦ = DMFₐᵦ − ψ(SMFₐ, SMFᵦ) ← ψ = additive/HSA null = f(1,1) − f(1,0) − f(0,1) + f(0,0) ← discrete ∂²f/∂a∂b (curvature) = [ f(1,1) − f(1,0) ] − [ f(0,1) − f(0,0) ] ← "how much more essential is b once a is lost"
The theorem that governs everything From the two scalars SMF_A and SMF_B you know three corners — f(0,0), f(1,0), f(0,1) — but not the (1,1) corner. GI is exactly the deviation of f(1,1) from the null. So predicting SL from two single GeneEffect scalars is not merely underpowered — under any fixed null it is identically zero (GI := DMF − ψ, and if your only guess for DMF is ψ, then GI ≡ 0). The missing datum is always the (1,1) corner, and it must come from somewhere.

What we tried, and why it paused — Bridge A vs measured K562 epistasis

Bridge A was run on the frozen exp05 backbone: feed the observed a-perturbed K562 cells as the basal state, re-query b's GeneEffect, symmetrize the co-dependency spike, and correlate against the measured Horlbeck 2018 K562 GI map (100,128 pairs; 83,028 covered).

The counterfactual shift is real (the model responds to the a-perturbed basal), but its signal-to-noise is ≈ 1.0: about half of each pair's score is cell-sampling noise. The composition mechanism is paused for redesign, not extended across contexts.

The reframe — why it failed, generalized to a design principle

  • The scalars carry none of the curvature — the state does. Bridge A works with singles only because it uses the a-perturbed transcriptome (does the network now route through b?), not the scalar SMF_A. The failure isn't "singles are too little."
  • Differencing first-order predictions to extract a second-order quantity is ill-conditioned. The readout collapsed a rich state to one scalar and then subtracted two noisy first-order predictions — SNR ≈ 1.0 is the signature — on the same K562-fit head that §1 showed collapses off-distribution.
  • Both bridges share one precondition: an accurate, non-collapsing backbone. That is exactly what §2 is building. So the composition redesign should ride the rebuilt backbone and change the readout — predict the interaction residual more directly, read a pathway-level co-dependency signal instead of a scalar, and use a paired estimator that cancels shared noise.
Where this leaves us The real axis is not singles vs doubles — it is what carries the curvature and how noisy the estimator is. Native Bridge B is a data dead-end in K562-KO right now; the state-based estimator is data-efficient but needs a better backbone and a direct-residual readout; a GI-label-calibrated head is the separate test of whether the signal exists at all. Direction for the redesign is the open item — §2's backbone is the shared prerequisite either way.
