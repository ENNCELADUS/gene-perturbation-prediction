Benchmarks and Metrics

Data of the two-stage task, 2026-11-04. Stage one predicts DepMap GeneEffect in held-out cell lines from their basal single-cell profiles. Stage two ranks the gene pairs of a screen by synthetic lethality (SL) in held-out lines.

1. Stage-one benchmark: GeneEffect in held-out lines

Task. For a held-out cell line c and a gene g, predict the DepMap CRISPR GeneEffect (release 26Q1) from the basal single-cell transcriptome of c. The model predicts the residual over the training mean of each gene. Thus the axis of generalization is the cell line.

Split. One fixed split of cell lines. All lines of one patient are on one side, and membership follows patient, lineage and expression source. Validation selects the model, and test is evaluated once.

2. Stage-two benchmark: SL pairs in held-out lines

Task. For a held-out line, rank the gene pairs that a screen tested in that line, so that the pairs the screen called synthetic lethal (SL) come first. The model reads only the line's basal profile and the gene pair.

58studies
310screens
135cell lines
55,147SL pairs
77lines kept in the benchmark

Sources. Published combinatorial double-knockout screens (173 screens) and anchor screens, in which one gene is lost and the rest of the genome is screened (137 screens). All labels come from experiments. A line enters the benchmark if it has basal single-cell profiles and screen labels; measured GeneEffect is optional.

Preprocessing

  1. Screen. A screen is the set of pairs that one study tested in one line with one setup. Each label belongs to one screen.
  2. Label. The authors' SL call. If the study has no call, the authors' cut on its score. If it has no cut, FDR < 0.05. A negative is a pair that the screen tested without an SL call.
  3. Pair. Two genes in alphabetical order, one row for each screen and pair. Same-gene pairs and control guides are removed. Where a study flags a gene as lethal alone, its negatives are left unlabelled.
  4. Conflicts. A pair can have different labels in two screens; both rows are kept.
  5. Condition. We keep the genetic screens in the benchmark: loss of function in the unperturbed parental line, which the basal profile describes. We drop the screens under a drug or irradiation, with an activating anchor, or with a call copied from a group of lines.
  6. Classes. We keep the screens with both SL and negative pairs and drop the others.

Split and protocol

  • Split. A fixed split of lines, independent of the stage-one split. We placed 17 validation and 16 test lines by hand, across lineages, so that training, validation and test hold about 80%, 10% and 10% of the kept SL pairs. Lines of one patient stay on one side.
  • Stage-one input. Stage two reads stage one's predicted GeneEffect from one fit on all lines with GeneEffect. Results are reported for the lines whose GeneEffect this fit reads (seen) and for the lines without GeneEffect (unseen).
  • Training. One model for all screens, with binary cross-entropy on the training lines. Each line has the same total weight, each study the same weight in its line, and each screen the same weight in its study. Inside a screen, the SL and the negative pairs each have half of the weight.
  • Selection. Validation selects the model with the metric below. Test is evaluated once.

3. Evaluation metrics

Stage one: per-line residual Spearman ↑

For each held-out line, the Spearman correlation over the selective genes between the predicted and the measured GeneEffect residual (GeneEffect minus the training gene mean, divided by the gene's residual SD). Selective genes have a dependency that changes across the training lines. Take the mean over lines. The gene mean predicts a zero residual, so its correlation is undefined. The metric asks which genes the line needs more or less than usual.

ρ = meanc Spearmang ∈ selective( rcg, r̂cg )

Stage two: recall in the top 10% ↑

The metric uses the known SL pairs, because a negative is only a screen non-hit. In each screen, rank the pairs and take the fraction of SL pairs in the top 10% (tied pairs share the credit; a random ranking gives 0.10). Take the mean over the screens of a study, then over the studies of a line, then over the lines. Thus each study has equal weight in its line, and each screen equal weight in its study.

R = meanc meant ∈ studies(c) means ∈ screens(t,c) recall@10%(s)

Controls. Pair identity (the pair's SL rate in the same study's training lines), pan-essentiality (the training mean GeneEffect of the two genes) and a context-ablated model (the same model with each gene's mean profile in place of the line's). The main result is the model minus pair identity for each line, with a 95% bootstrap interval over lines. AUROC and AUPRC over the SL rate are secondary metrics.
