Benchmarks and Metrics

Data of the two-stage task, 2026-11-04. Stage one predicts DepMap GeneEffect in held-out cell lines from their basal single-cell profiles. Stage two ranks the gene pairs of a screen by synthetic lethality (SL) in held-out lines.

1. Stage-one benchmark: GeneEffect in held-out lines

Task. For a held-out cell line c and a gene g, predict the DepMap CRISPR GeneEffect (release 26Q1) from the basal single-cell transcriptome of c. The model predicts the residual over the training mean of each gene. Thus the cell line, not the gene, is the axis of generalization.

Split. One fixed split of cell lines. No patient has lines on two sides, and GeneEffect values did not set membership. Validation selects the model, and test is scored once.

2. Stage-two benchmark: SL pairs in held-out lines

Task. For a held-out line, rank the gene pairs that a screen tested in that line, so that the pairs the screen called synthetic lethal (SL) come first. The model reads the line's basal profile and the gene pair. It does not read the screen or the study.

58studies
310screens
135cell lines
55,147SL pairs
76lines in the main result

Sources. Published combinatorial double-knockout screens (173 screens) and anchor screens, in which one gene is lost and the rest of the genome is screened (137 screens). Computational predictions are not used. A line enters the benchmark if it has basal single-cell profiles and screen labels. It does not need measured GeneEffect.

Preprocessing

  1. Screen. A screen is the set of pairs that one study tested in one line with one setup. Labels stay inside their screen and are not merged across screens or studies.
  2. Label. The authors' SL call. If the study has no call, the authors' cut on its score. If it has no cut, FDR < 0.05. A negative is a pair that the screen tested and did not call. It is not a proven non-SL pair.
  3. Pair. Two genes in alphabetical order, one row for each screen and pair. Same-gene pairs and control guides are removed. Where a study flags a gene as lethal alone, its negatives are left unlabelled.
  4. Conflicts. A pair can be SL in one screen and not SL in another screen. Both rows are kept.
  5. Condition. The main result uses genetic screens: loss of function in the unperturbed parental line, which the basal profile describes. Screens under a drug or irradiation, with an activating anchor, or with a call copied from a group of lines are kept out of the main result.
  6. Classes. A screen with no SL pair or no negative pair is not scored.

Split and protocol

  • Split. A fixed random split of the 85 lines with basal profiles and labels, about 60% training, 20% validation and 20% test, drawn within each lineage. The held-out lines of stage one keep their side. Lines of one patient stay on one side.
  • Stage-one input. Stage two reads stage one's predicted GeneEffect from one fit on all lines with GeneEffect. A held-out SL line without GeneEffect is therefore never in that fit.
  • Training. One model for all screens, with binary cross-entropy on the training lines. Each line has the same total weight, each study the same weight in its line, and each screen the same weight in its study. Inside a screen, the SL and the negative pairs each have half of the weight.
  • Selection. Validation selects the model with the metric below. Test is scored once.

3. Evaluation metrics

Stage one: per-line residual Spearman ↑

For each held-out line, the Spearman correlation over the selective genes between the predicted and the measured GeneEffect residual (GeneEffect minus the training gene mean, divided by the gene's residual SD). Selective genes have a dependency that changes across the training lines. Take the mean over lines. The gene mean predicts no residual, so it has no score. The metric asks which genes the line needs more or less than usual.

ρ = meanc Spearmang ∈ selective( rcg, r̂cg )

Stage two: recall in the top 10% ↑

The negatives are tested and not called, so the metric uses only the known SL pairs. In each screen, rank the pairs and take the fraction of SL pairs in the top 10% (tied pairs share the credit; a random ranking gives 0.10). Take the mean over the screens of a study, then over the studies of a line, then over the lines. Thus a large screen, or a study with many screens, does not control a line.

R = meanc meant ∈ studies(c) means ∈ screens(t,c) recall@10%(s)

Controls. Pair identity (the pair's SL rate in the same study's training lines), pan-essentiality (the training mean GeneEffect of the two genes) and a context-ablated model (the same model with each gene's mean profile in place of the line's). The main result is the model minus pair identity for each line, with a 95% bootstrap interval over lines. AUROC and AUPRC over the SL rate are secondary metrics.
