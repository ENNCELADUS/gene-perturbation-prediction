## 1. Named readouts: explicit models and what they identify

### 1.1 Chronos

Dempster et al., *Genome Biology* 22:343 (2021), "Chronos: a cell population dynamics model of CRISPR experiments that improves inference of gene fitness effects." DOI `10.1186/s13059-021-02540-7`, PMID 34930405, PMC8686573. Retrieved: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8686573/

**Explicit model.** Chronos models sgRNA-carrying cell counts as a two-population mixture per cell line/sgRNA:

```
N_cj(t) = N_cj(0) * [ p_c*p_j * e^(R_cg* * t)  +  (1 − p_c*p_j) * e^(R_c * t) ]
```

where `p_c`, `p_j` are per-line/per-guide knockout-efficacy probabilities, `R_c` is the unperturbed (single, net) growth rate, and `R_cg*` is the growth rate of the successfully-knocked-out subpopulation. **There is exactly one growth-rate parameter per state (`R_c`, `R_cg*`)** — an exponential net rate, not a birth-rate/death-rate pair.

**Identified quantity.** The reported "gene fitness effect" is `r_cg = (R_cg*/R_c) − 1`, i.e., the *fractional change in the single net growth rate* attributable to complete knockout. Identification requires a pDNA/very-early reference timepoint, hyperparameter constraints on the knockout-delay term (default 3 days), and copy-number correction anchored to a common-essential-gene set.

**Can it separate reduced division from increased loss?** No, and the reason is structural, not a missing analysis choice: the state equation contains only `e^(R*t)` — a single net exponential rate — so the sgRNA-abundance likelihood is a function of `R_cg*` alone. Division and death enter only through their algebraic difference; no combination of observed sgRNA counts at any number of timepoints can separate them under this model, because the model itself has no free parameter for death independent of division. The paper does not explicitly flag this decomposition failure (it discusses knockout-*penetrance* ambiguity — incomplete vs. complete knockout producing the same depletion — which is a related but distinct identifiability problem, not the division/death one). **This is an assumption failure the paper is silent on, not a limitation it argues around.**

### 1.2 GR metrics (Hafner et al. 2016)

Hafner, Niepel, Chung, Sorger, *Nature Methods* 13:521–527 (2016), "Growth rate inhibition metrics correct for confounders in measuring sensitivity to cancer drugs." DOI `10.1038/nmeth.3853`, PMID 27135972, PMC4887336. Retrieved: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/4887336/

**Inputs.** Endpoint form needs `x0` (seeding count), `x_ctrl(T)`, and `x(c,T)` (treated count at one later time `T`); a time-resolved form uses cell counts at several ~12–18 h intervals (about half a division time) instead.

**Definition.** `GR(c) = 2^(log2(x(c)/x0)/log2(x_ctrl/x0)) − 1`, fit to a Hill-type curve giving GR50 / GRmax / GEC50.

**What it distinguishes, and how.** `GR = 0` is defined as complete cytostasis; `GR ∈ (0,1)` partial inhibition; `GR ∈ (−1,0)` net cell loss. **This is explicitly a statement about net population doubling, not a separated birth/death pair.** The paper's own underlying ODE is `ẋ = x·k·(1 − SM·c^h/(SC50^h+c^h)) − x·(kL·c^h/(LC50^h+c^h))`, which *does* contain a distinct untreated-growth term `k` and an optional killing term `kL` — so the *generative model* has a place for a death-like parameter. But the GR statistic itself, as computed from cell-count timepoints alone, only ever reports the **net** exponent; `kL` and the cytostatic component of `k`'s suppression are not separately recovered from count data unless additional information (e.g., a death-specific assay) is layered on. The paper states GR values become independent of untreated growth rate `k` specifically "for cases where drug action is mainly related to the cell cycle (`kL=0`)" — a special case, not a general decomposition.

**Assumptions.** Exponential proliferation, drug effect stabilized by first division, homogeneous population (single division-time parameter per condition).

**Verdict for the spec's question:** GR/DIP-style metrics distinguish *net* cytostasis from *net* cytotoxicity given a time course and initial counts, exactly as the spec anticipated — but they do **not**, on their own, identify a death rate separable from a division rate.

### 1.3 DIP rate (Harris et al. 2016)

Harris, Kell (and Quaranta group), *Nature Methods* 13:497–498 (2016), "An unbiased metric of antiproliferative drug effect in vitro." DOI `10.1038/nmeth.3852`, PMID 27135974, PMC4887341. Retrieved: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4887341

**Definition.** DIP rate = slope of `log2(cell number)` vs. time in the steady-state (post-lag) window, from live-cell fluorescence imaging (H2B-mRFP nuclear counting) over ≥5 days.

**Explicit statement on decomposition.** The paper states the drug "modulates the difference between the rates of these two processes [division and death], i.e., the net rate of proliferation" and provides **no decomposition** — no combination with an independent death assay is discussed. This is the same structural limitation as GR: net rate only, by explicit textual acknowledgment.

### 1.4 Death-seq

Colville, Liu, et al., *Cell Metabolism* 35:1976–1992 (2023), "Death-seq identifies regulators of cell death and senolytic therapies." DOI `10.1016/j.cmet.2023.08.008`, PMID 37699398, PMC10597643. Retrieved: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10597643/

**What it identifies.** A **positive-selection** screen: dying/detaching cells are physically collected (via centrifugation of media containing detached cells) and their sgRNA distribution is compared directly against the surviving-attached population. This measures death **directly**, not by depletion inference — its explicit rationale is that dropout/negative-selection screens are underpowered for death because of short death timescales and poor signal-to-noise in non-dividing populations.

**Scope limit.** Death-seq is fundamentally a **death-enhancer/suppressor discovery screen under a context** — it was run paired with senescence induction (with a genome-wide screen finding enhancers of senescent death even *without* an added pharmacologic agent, at days 7 and 9 post-senescence-induction) or with a drug. It reports casTLE confidence scores / hit calls (categorical, with continuous confidence), **not a continuous, cross-gene-comparable death *rate***, and it does **not** decompose division suppression from death enhancement for an arbitrary single-gene perturbation on its own — it tells you a gene's knockout increases the *dying* population's representation under the tested context, which is a different (narrower, but directly death-specific) quantity than a birth/death rate pair for that gene in an unperturbed-context growth assay.

### 1.5 Live-seq

Chen, Guillaume-Gentil, et al., *Nature* 608:733–740 (2022), "Live-seq enables temporal transcriptomic recording of single cells." DOI `10.1038/s41586-022-05046-9`, PMID 35978187, PMC9402441. Retrieved (PMC full text, nature.com blocked by login wall): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9402441/

**Throughput.** ~4–5 extractions/hour by fluidic-force-microscopy biopsy; 641 biopsy samples across 5 replicates, 588 sequencing libraries, 294 passing QC; the paired same-cell (before/after) prospective design was demonstrated on small numbers per replicate (e.g., sequential RAW macrophage sampling: 24 extracted → 4 paired quality-passing transcriptomes; sequential ASPC sampling: 44 extracted → 8 paired profiles). **Total high-quality single-cell transcriptomes across the whole study ≈ 300.** This is orders of magnitude below Perturb-seq/pooled-screen scale.

**Cell types.** RAW264.7 macrophages (and an mCherry-reporter subline), immortalized brown preadipocytes (IBA), and primary adipose stromal/progenitor cells (ASPCs). No cancer cell line, no K562.

**Does the biopsy perturb fate?** Benchmarked: post-biopsy viability 85–89% across the three cell types (vs. 90–95% for standard dissociation controls); only 12 genes were significantly differentially expressed among control/1h/4h post-extraction conditions in IBA cells; extracted RAW cells recovered pre-extraction volume within 100–320 min and divided on a timescale similar to non-extracted controls. **This is a real, quantified, small perturbation, not zero perturbation** — a caveat the deliverable must retain when citing Live-seq as "non-destructive."

**CRISPR coupling.** None found. The paper uses only fluorescent reporters (mCherry/BFP/GFP), a FUCCI-type cell-cycle indicator, and lentiviral barcoding for ASPC clone tracking — no sgRNA/knockout/knockdown anywhere in the text.

**Same-cell prospective design (the A1 existence proof).** LPS/macrophage experiment: cells are Live-seq–biopsied, then immediately imaged live for *Tnf*-mCherry response to LPS over 8+ h — ground-state transcriptome of a cell is coupled to that **same** cell's own downstream reporter kinetics. ASPC experiment: cells are extracted once, tracked, extracted a **second time** after 2 days of differentiation induction, then followed to day 7 to confirm retained differentiation (lipid droplets) in the *same*, twice-biopsied cells. Both are genuine A1-tier (same-cell prospective) designs — but neither is a genetic perturbation, and neither is a cancer-dependency-relevant system.

**Reading across this table:** the only combinations that jointly identify division rate *and* death rate as separate, comparable-across-conditions quantities are (a) direct single-cell event-calling imaging (FUCCI+caspase/Annexin, when tracking is maintained), and (b) formal stochastic/phylodynamic inference from either population-count variance (Chen et al. 2024) or lineage trees (Pilarski et al. 2026) — and in both inference-based cases, **death-rate recovery is markedly weaker and more bias-prone than division-rate recovery**, an asymmetry that recurs across every count-based or tree-based method found. Label-dilution/barcode methods (Watermelon, LARRY) identify division well but explicitly do **not** resolve death from non-sampling.

## 3. MNAR / observation process: what is knowable, and whether bounds exist

**Two distinct problems must not be merged** (the literature conflates them more than the spec allows):
1. **Cell-level MNAR** — a whole cell is absent from the final dataset because it died/lysed/was lost before or during capture. This is the spec's concern (§6).
2. **Gene-level dropout** — within a *captured* cell, a gene's transcript fails detection (technical zero vs. biological zero). This is a separate, better-studied statistical problem (e.g., PMC6215955, PMC4270922 on MNAR dropout models) and imputation methods (ADImpute, PMC8890719) address *this*, not whole-cell loss. **No imputation method recovers a cell that was never captured.**

**Evidence found on cell-level, state-dependent loss:**

- Denisenko et al., *Genome Biology* 21:130 (2020), "Systematic assessment of tissue dissociation and storage biases in single-cell and single-nucleus RNA-seq workflows." DOI `10.1186/s13059-020-02048-6`, PMID 32487174, PMC7265231. (Retrieved at WebSearch/abstract level; not deep-read via WebFetch in this session — **marked abstract-only**.) Reports that warm (37 °C) dissociation induces an artifactual stress signature ("regulation of cell death" as the top enriched GO term in genes upregulated by warm vs. cold dissociation) and that cryopreservation causes differential loss of specific cell types (e.g., epithelial cells), while methanol fixation preserves cell-type composition better but introduces ambient RNA leakage. **This shows dissociation protocol choice itself creates state-dependent (not random) cell loss and stress signatures that can be mistaken for genuine death signaling** — directly relevant to, but not conclusive proof of, the spec's warning against reading mito-rich/stress clusters as "the dying population."

- Hong et al., *Nature Communications* 13:1688 (2022), "Comprehensive generation, visualization, and reporting of quality control metrics for single-cell RNA sequencing data." DOI `10.1038/s41467-022-29212-9`, PMID 35354805, PMC8967915. Retrieved: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8967915/. This is **primarily a QC-tooling/infrastructure paper** (the singleCellTK QC pipeline), not a mechanistic study. It does contain the load-bearing sentence the spec relies on: cells stressed during dissociation "may express abnormally large proportions of mitochondrial genes" and "may appear as a unique cluster in the scRNA-seq data even though they did not represent a unique cell population in the original tissue sample." **This is exactly the claim boundary the spec draws (§6, §12): a mito-rich cluster is a sample-preparation artifact candidate, not automatically a recovered dying population** — but the paper itself proposes no method to tell the two apart; it flags the risk without resolving it.

- Yates, Kraft, Boeva, *Genome Biology* 26:99 (2025), "Filtering cells with high mitochondrial content depletes viable metabolically altered malignant cell populations in cancer single-cell studies." DOI `10.1186/s13059-025-03559-w`, PMID 40205439, PMC11983838. Retrieved: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11983838/. Across 9 cancer scRNA-seq datasets (~441,000 cells, 134 patients — lung, small-cell lung, renal, breast, prostate, nasopharyngeal, uveal melanoma, pancreatic; **no K562/leukemia line found in this study**), high-mitochondrial-fraction malignant cells show **inconsistent** dissociation-stress-signature elevation across cohorts (only 3/9 meta-analyses showed elevation, effect size small), a bulk-vs-single-cell paired comparison finding only 1/23 and 1/9 patients (two cohorts) with dissociation-attributable elevated mito residuals, and spatial transcriptomics (Visium HD) showing high-mito malignant regions with normal tissue morphology in situ. Standard 15% mito-fraction cutoffs would remove a large, cohort-variable fraction of malignant cells (10–50% of malignant populations flagged HighMT across many samples). **No direct viability assay (FACS live/dead, trypan blue, PI) is reported** — the case rests entirely on indirect anchors. The authors themselves note they could not fully rule out the QC-relaxation-recovers-an-artifact concern (limited access to unfiltered raw data), so **this paper corroborates the spec's caution rather than resolving it**: it is strong evidence that high-mito ≠ death in cancer cells specifically, but it does not supply a general quantitative bound.

**Do bounds exist?** No general, quantitative bound on the fraction of state-dependent pre-capture cell loss was found in this search. What exists instead:
- Qualitative demonstrations that the confound is real and cell-type/protocol-dependent (Denisenko et al.; Yates et al.).
- Protocol mitigations that *reduce* differential loss without eliminating it: cold dissociation reduces the warm-dissociation stress signature; methanol/glyoxal fixation preserves cell-type composition and transcriptomes of already-fixed stressed cells better than live-sort pipelines (methanol fixation shows high correlation with live-cell profiles, "does not significantly alter" gene/transcript counts per cell per search-level sources — **abstract/protocol-level, not deep-read**) — but fixation only helps for cells that survive to the fixation step; a cell that lysed or was mechanically lost earlier is still never observed, by any protocol.
- Single-nucleus RNA-seq changes *which* cell types are captured (e.g., underrepresents lymphocytes relative to scRNA-seq per search-level sources) rather than removing the state-dependence; it trades one selection profile for another, not for an unselected one.
- No paper was found proposing an explicit MNAR statistical model for whole-cell, state-dependent loss in a perturbation/death context (the MNAR-modeling literature located, e.g. PMC6215955 and PMC4270922, addresses gene-level dropout or clinical-trial dropout, not single-cell state-dependent whole-cell loss).

**Per the non-negotiable rule that absence of evidence is not falsification:** the honest statement is *"no bound was found,"* not *"no bound exists."* This is consistent with, and does not contradict, the spec's own withdrawn-claim list (§6): "independence between removal rate and perturbation strength does not bound pre-capture loss" remains correct and unrebutted by anything found here.

| Mito-fraction / QC-metric-based "dying cell" inference | Single cell (QC metric) | **Nothing reliably** — shown in cancer cells specifically to often reflect a viable altered metabolic state, not death | None hold reliably across contexts; explicitly warned against as a definitional dying-cell signature | Neither — this is a claim boundary, not a usable identified quantity | Cannot support any tier; using it degrades a claim, not strengthens it |

## Gate 1 verdict

**Candidate A (lineage/clone level): `proceed`, narrowed to the A2 ceiling already fixed at L0.**
Credible independent anchors exist: barcode + label-dilution designs (Watermelon) and barcode-split state/fate designs (LARRY) both instantiate real, demonstrated A2-tier evidence, and Bayesian lineage-phylodynamic inference (Pilarski et al. 2026) can in principle recover a division rate (well) and a death rate (weakly, with known bias) from CRISPR-scar lineage trees. None of this triggers the no-go clause, because identification through a credible independent anchor is possible — it is simply capped at clone-level association, never per-cell fate, exactly as revision 3 already stipulates. The open item is **not** an identifiability failure but a **data-availability gap**: no existing dataset combines CRISPR(i) perturbation + early/multi-timepoint sampling + lineage barcoding + cancer/dependency-relevant cell line. This is a resourcing/assay-adaptation question (transplant the LARRY-split or Watermelon label-dilution design onto a K562 CRISPRi experiment), which the spec's §9 permits as a bounded, decision-enabling feasibility investigation before Gate 2 — it does not by itself block proceeding past Gate 1.

**Candidate B (population level): `proceed`.**
A rank-1 condition-level paired prospective anchor (state at `t0`, dynamics over `[t0,T]`) is assemblable now from existing, independently demonstrated components: time-resolved GR/DIP-style count tracking (or the richer birth-death stochastic inference of Chen et al. 2024, which separately identifies both rates from count-variance) combined with an independent death-specific readout (Death-seq-style detachment collection, or Annexin V/PI time course), anchored against an independent net-fitness measurement (Chronos/DepMap) per the spec's Analysis-P temporal contract. No fundamental identifiability wall was found. The MNAR/mito-QC literature (Denisenko et al.; Hong et al.; Yates et al.) constrains *which* readouts are trustworthy (ruling out mito-fraction-based death inference, and prohibiting "QC-relaxation recovered the dying population" claims) but does not prevent the target estimand from being defined and identified through readouts that do not rely on that confounded proxy.

**Neither candidate triggers a stop.** Per the spec's explicit instruction, the existence of good independent decompositions (GR/DIP, birth-death inference, phylodynamics) is *the ground truth the prospective question requires* — it is evidence the measurement problem is tractable, not a reason to stop.
