Multi-Study SL Benchmark: Baselines

Run benchmark_baselines_20261104 on the H20 (port 30838), code 9d9f117, seed 0. Data card: SL benchmark.

Question

Stage two ranks the SL partners of a gene in a held-out cell line. Before a stage-two head is trained, this run asks how far simple scorers go on the multi-study benchmark, and whether stage one's predicted GeneEffect adds anything to a gene's mean.

Benchmark

  • 58 studies, 310 lists (a list is the pairs one study tested in one line), 135 lines. 82 lines have prepared basal scRNA, a side in the SL split and a list with both classes: 50 train, 16 validation, 16 test.
  • The SL split (stage two only) draws lines with and without GeneEffect. Validation: 14 lines with GeneEffect and 2 without; test: 13 and 3. A screened line that shares a patient with a drawn line goes to the same side.
  • A list's negatives are tested and not called; they are not true negatives. A row is scored only where every scorer can read both genes, so the positive counts below are smaller than the lists' counts.

Rows

  • Stage one, production fit: the default linear context prior (the row the stage-one benchmark chose), fitted once on all 1001 labelled lines with no validation set. It predicts GeneEffect for every single-cell line: in sample for the lines with GeneEffect, out of sample for the 17 lines without. It leaves out HAP1, because HAP1 shares a patient with eHAP (an SL test line without GeneEffect). The score of a pair is minus the sum of the two genes' predicted GeneEffect.
  • Pan-essentiality: the same sum with each gene's mean GeneEffect over the SL training lines.
  • Pair identity (control): the pair's hit rate in the same study's training lines.
  • Measured GeneEffect (reference, not a model): the line's own DepMap GeneEffect; only lines with GeneEffect have it.

Reading

  • Pair identity wins on every group with enough lines. On test, all lines: 0.465 against 0.268 for stage one; the gap is −0.209 [−0.339, −0.071]. Most of the benchmark's signal is the same pairs called again in other lines.
  • Stage one's prediction adds nothing over the gene mean. Its recall is within 0.02 of pan-essentiality on every group, and the measured GeneEffect does no better.
  • The lines without GeneEffect give no conclusion. They are 2 validation and 3 test lines (446 test positives). Stage two trains mostly on in-sample stage-one inputs, so the number for this group is a low estimate; the group is small and mostly non-cancer.
