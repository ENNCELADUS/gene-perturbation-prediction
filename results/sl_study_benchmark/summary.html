Multi-study SL benchmark: labels, split and baselines

This page records the first build of the multi-study SL benchmark and its first baselines. Every number comes from the host files copied to results/sl_study_benchmark/host/. Seed 0. Nothing here is SL context evidence.

The question

Stage two ranks SL partners in a held-out cell line. The three-screen benchmark has few lines. This benchmark takes per-line labels from every kept SL screen study instead (design, data card).

The unit is a list: the pairs that one study tested in one line, or in one anchor comparison of one line. Labels are never merged across studies. The baselines show how much of a list's ranking comes from context-blind knowledge before any model reads the line's profile.

The benchmark

The rows

Each row gives one score per pair, and a higher score means a more likely SL pair. For a combinatorial list the score sums both genes. An anchor list is ranked by gene B alone, because gene A (a gene or a drug) is the same for the whole list.

Pan-essentiality and pair identity are context-blind controls. Pair identity is undefined for a study with no training line, so it scores fewer lists. The default linear prior predicts the line's GeneEffect from its expression, refitted on the SL split's training side. Measured GeneEffect is the line's own CRISPR screen. It is a reference, not a model, because the task gives a held-out line no screen.

What ran

All steps ran on the H20 container on port 30838, on the CPU, from branch feat/sl-study-benchmark.

Results

The metric is the AUPRC of a list's ranking divided by the list's positive rate (1 is a random ranking). The headline averages the lists of each line, then averages the lines, so a line that many studies tested counts once.

Reading

  • Pair identity is the strongest row where it applies (Tables 5–6). Its test mean is heavy-tailed: one Zhang2026 list in A549 has 4 positives in 98,852 pairs and a lift of 4,943, and it sets most of the mean. A list with very few positives makes the lift unstable; the minimum positives per list is still undecided (design §4).
  • The prior adds little to pan-essentiality (Table 7). The paired gain is +0.05 [-0.16, +0.22] on validation and +0.12 [+0.04, +0.24] on test, against lifts near 3 to 6.
  • Measured GeneEffect beats pan-essentiality on validation but not on test (Table 7). Even the line's own screen does not reliably beat each gene's mean, so a context model has little room above pan-essentiality on these lists.
  • The held-out sides are small. Each side has 12 lines, and validation has 16 lists, so the intervals are wide. The 17 rule rows that need a decision and the lines with no scRNA would add lists.
